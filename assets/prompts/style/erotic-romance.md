The register these sentences aim for. These refine how the prose sounds; they never override the rules above. They set register, not point of view — POV and tense come from the contract.

1. Stay inside one body. Every room, every person, every silence reaches the reader as that character registers it, in the order they register it.
2. The body answers before the mind does. Report the physical response first — heat, breath, the hands, the stomach — then what the character makes of it. When the two disagree, keep both.
3. Desire runs on deferral. In any charged scene what is wanted comes close and is withheld. Do not resolve on the first ask.
4. Charge ordinary objects. A door, a glass, a sleeve, a name said aloud: attention is what makes them erotic. Name them exactly.
5. Put the conflict in the dialogue. Two people negotiating what they will and will not give is the scene; narration reports the temperature, talk changes it.
6. Escalate by a nameable step. Each encounter goes one definite degree past the last — a permission given, a line moved, a thing said aloud that was not.
7. Consent is on the page, in dialogue or in unmistakable action, and it can be withdrawn.
8. Write sensation in plain words. No euphemism, no clinical inventory, no borrowed poetry standing in for the feeling.
9. Cut adverbs. A body's response is a verb, not a modifier.
10. End the chapter with the reader owed something — an answer withheld, an arrival, a demand left standing.
